## Known Scanning Ambiguities
| Question | Issue | Assumption Stated in Guide |
|----------|-------|----------------------------|
| A1-Q4 | Right end of R3 cut off; no closed loop through it | Under common-bottom assumption gives negative resistance — physically impossible; missing connection must be supplied by teacher |
| A1-Q5 | 20 V polarity and 2 A arrow direction unclear | Assumes 20 V positive at top and 2 A arrow right; current flows from 20 V node toward junction |
| A2-Q1 | 13 A arrow and 50 V polarity unclear | Conditional: 7/3 A ≈ 2.33 A if 13 A downward and 50 V positive at top |
| A2-Q2 | 20 V source in series with 10 Ω branch | −79/47 A ≈ −1.68 A under stated polarities (10 V and 20 V both positive at top) |
| June 2025 Q4(a) | 5 A source arrow faint | Two possible answers depending on arrow direction: 0.75 A B→A (upward) or 1.75 A A→B (downward) |

## OCR Recommendations
1. **Always redraw the circuit** in the exam and mark source polarities and current arrow directions before writing equations. Scanned copies may have faint or ambiguous markings.
2. **Never memorise a numerical answer** without the accompanying polarity/arrow assumptions. The scan quality does not guarantee consistent polarity across batches.
3. **For A1-Q4:** the physical circuit is underdetermined without the teacher's supplied connection. Do not attempt a numerical answer.
4. **For June 2025 Q4(a):** copy the 5 A source arrow direction from your paper onto your answer sheet; the two possible readings differ by 1 A.
5. **Cross-check** every final answer by substituting back into one KCL or KVL equation. This is the single most effective error-detection habit.
